How much does a website cost in the UK?

Real 2026 price ranges for business sites, e-commerce and web apps, and the decisions that move a quote up or down.

By Maksim Ilin, Technical Lead at ICE WIND · 15+ years in software engineering · Updated 10 August 2026

Most UK businesses pay between £1,500 and £8,000 for a professionally built website in 2026. E-commerce stores run £4,500 to £15,000, and custom web products with logins, dashboards and payments start around £8,000 and climb past £25,000.

Those are wide ranges. The gap between the bottom and the top comes down to a handful of decisions you control, and this guide walks through them: prices by project type, what moves a quote up, and the ongoing costs that surprise people a year after launch.

What drives the price up

Four things move a website quote more than anything else.

Page count comes first. Ten pages need ten designs, ten rounds of copy and ten passes of testing, and professional copywriting alone adds £500 to £2,000 on most projects.

Custom design is the second lever. A template skin on WordPress or Shopify costs a fraction of a design built around your customers. Custom web design pays off when you need to stand out or convert cold traffic. A template works fine for a site people only visit after meeting you.

E-commerce mechanics add the next chunk: product pages, basket, checkout, payments, shipping rules, VAT handling. Each one is real build work, which is why store projects start thousands above brochure sites.

Integrations are the wildcard. Connecting a CRM, booking system or accounting tool through an off-the-shelf plugin usually costs little, while custom API work adds £500 to £3,000 or more per integration. Scope this early. Unplanned integrations cause more mid-project price surprises than anything else on this list.

Freelancer, agency or DIY builder?

A DIY builder such as Wix or Squarespace costs £10 to £50 a month and suits a side project or a placeholder while you validate an idea. You pay with your own evenings and a site that looks like its template.

A freelancer typically charges £1,200 to £2,000 for a standard four to five page site in the UK. Good freelancers are excellent value. The risks are availability and breadth: one person rarely covers design, build, SEO and ongoing support equally well.

An agency or studio charges more because you get a process and more than one specialist. UK and US senior developer time bills at roughly $120 to $200 an hour according to OneLittleWeb's 2026 pricing study, and a studio project buys design, development, QA and launch support as a package. Before hiring anyone, check the company is real: registration at Companies House, insurance, an ICO registration if they handle personal data. We publish ours on our trust page and think every UK studio should.

Did AI make websites cheaper in 2026?

Yes and no. AI tooling cut the hours behind repetitive work: boilerplate code, first-draft layouts, test coverage. At ICE WIND we build AI-native, which is a real part of why our landing page price starts at £1,500 rather than £3,000. The saving shows up in delivery speed too. Work that took six weeks in 2023 often ships in three.

The parts AI did not make cheaper are the parts that decide whether the site works: understanding your customers, structuring the offer, writing pages that sell, and the judgement calls during build. Fully AI-generated sites exist, cost almost nothing, and read like it. If the site needs to win you customers, the human layer is still the product. The same logic applies to AI automation projects: API calls cost pennies, and you pay a specialist to work out which workflow deserves them.

The ongoing costs people forget to budget

A website costs money after launch. Plan for these from day one:

  • Domain: £10 – £20 a year
  • Hosting: from a few pounds a month for a static site to £50+ for busy stores
  • Maintenance and small changes: £30 – £300 a month on a typical care plan
  • Licences: premium plugins, fonts, email tools

Across UK guides, £500 to £2,000 a year is a realistic running total for a small business site. Static sites sit at the bottom of that range, which is one reason we default to them for web development projects that do not need a heavy CMS.

How to get an accurate quote

Vague briefs get padded quotes. Agencies price risk, so the less you specify, the more margin they add. Write one page covering your goal, a couple of reference sites, the pages or features you need, your deadline and your budget band. With that in hand, a studio can give you a fixed price instead of a range. At ICE WIND we fix the cost once scope is agreed and split payment 50% at start, 50% before delivery. Send us a short brief through start a project and you will get a scoped quote back, plus the clearest next step even if that step is smaller than you expected.

FAQ

Common questions about website costs

Can I get a professional website for under £1,000 in the UK?
From an experienced freelancer or studio, rarely. Under £1,000 usually buys a template setup with your logo and text dropped in. That can be the right call for a placeholder site, but budget £1,500+ for custom work.
How long does a website take to build?
A landing page takes one to two weeks. A 5–10 page business site takes three to five weeks. E-commerce and web apps run four to twelve weeks depending on integrations. Content is the usual bottleneck, so start writing early.
Why do agencies charge more than freelancers?
You pay for a team and a process: designer, developer, QA and project management instead of one generalist. For simple sites a freelancer is often the better buy. For projects with integrations, e-commerce or deadlines that matter, the premium buys reliability.
What ongoing costs should I expect after launch?
Domain, hosting, licences and maintenance. Budget £500 to £2,000 a year for a small business site. Ask for the ongoing cost estimate in writing before you sign the build contract.
Is VAT included in UK website quotes?
Usually not. UK agencies quote ex-VAT, so add 20% if the supplier is VAT-registered and you cannot reclaim it. Ask directly, as this changes a £5,000 quote into £6,000.
