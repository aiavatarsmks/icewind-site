How much does AI automation cost for a small business?

Real 2026 prices for workflows, chatbots and assistants, where the payback comes from, and which project to buy first.

By Maksim Ilin, Technical Lead at ICE WIND · 15+ years in software engineering · Updated 11 August 2026

A single automated workflow costs £900 to £2,500 from a UK studio in 2026 and takes one to two weeks to build. A custom AI assistant that knows your business runs £2,500 to £10,000. Ongoing support, if you want it, adds £900 to £1,750 a month.

Those numbers cover the projects small businesses buy most. The wide gap between a £900 workflow and a £10,000 assistant comes down to scope, and this guide explains where the money goes, what the payback looks like, and which project to buy first.

A workflow automation is one process, taken end to end: an enquiry that gets qualified, logged and answered; an invoice that gets read, matched and filed; a report that assembles itself every Monday morning. An assistant is a bigger build: it holds your company knowledge, answers questions from staff or customers, and needs guardrails, monitoring and a proper handover.

Independent UK price guides sit in the same range. AI Workforce puts small-business automation projects between a few hundred pounds a month for tools and £1,000 to £10,000 for custom builds. If someone quotes you £30,000 to “transform your business with AI”, ask which single process they will fix first.

Where AI automation is spreading in the UK

Adoption has moved fast. ONS figures put 25% of UK businesses on AI by late 2025, up from 10% two years earlier, and among engaged SMEs the British Chambers of Commerce found 54% using it by March 2026. Marketing and admin lead the use cases: 72% of adopting firms automate there first, according to DSIT’s 2025 research.

The same research carries a warning for anyone about to spend money. 75% of adopters report productivity gains, but only 12% report increased revenue. Automation buys back hours. Whether those hours turn into money depends on what you do with them, and no supplier can promise that part for you.

What drives the price up

Integrations move an AI automation quote more than anything else. A workflow that lives inside one tool stays near the bottom of the range. Every extra system it touches, your CRM, your accounting package, a booking calendar, a legacy database, adds £750 to £2,000 of integration work. Scope these before you sign, because an integration discovered mid-project costs more than one planned from the start.

Data quality is the second lever. AI works with what you give it. If your product information lives in six spreadsheets with three naming conventions, someone pays to clean that up first, either in the project price or in months of wrong answers.

The third is the cost of a mistake. An automation that drafts internal reports can fail cheaply. One that emails your customers or touches invoices needs review steps, logging and guardrails, and that engineering is where a £900 workflow becomes a £2,500 one. We build the checks in from the start rather than bolting them on after the first bad send.

Running costs stay small by comparison. The API calls behind a typical small-business workflow cost pennies to a few pounds a day. You pay a specialist to work out which workflow deserves them, a point we made in our guide to website costs in the UK, and it holds even more for automation.

Which project to buy first

Buy one workflow, not a platform. The businesses that get value from AI in 2026 started with a single process that hurt every week, measured the hours it gave back, then added the next one. The businesses that stalled bought a six-month “AI transformation” and are still in workshops.

Pick the process with three properties: it happens often, it follows rules you can write down, and a mistake in it is cheap to catch. Enquiry handling, quote drafting, invoice matching and weekly reporting all qualify for most firms. Hiring decisions and anything touching payroll do not.

One more filter: the process should already work manually. Automation speeds up a working process. It cannot fix a broken one, and paying £2,000 to automate chaos gets you faster chaos.

The questions to ask any supplier

Ask what happens when the AI is wrong, because it will be sometimes. A serious supplier shows you the review step, the logs and the fallback. A seller of magic changes the subject.

Ask who owns the prompts, the workflow definitions and the data. The answer should be you, in writing. UK GDPR applies to automated processing the same as to any other kind, and if the supplier handles personal data they should hold an ICO registration. We publish ours, along with company registration and insurance, on our trust page.

Ask for the monthly running cost in writing: API usage, hosting, tool licences. For a typical single workflow that lands between £20 and £150 a month, and you want that number before you sign, not on the first invoice.

FAQ

Common questions about AI automation costs

What is the cheapest useful AI automation?
A single workflow in the £900 to £1,500 band: enquiry qualification, document intake or a self-assembling weekly report. One or two integrations, a review step, delivered inside two weeks.
Can I do this myself with ChatGPT?
For drafting and one-off tasks, yes, and you should. Paying for a build makes sense when the task repeats, touches your systems and needs to run without you prompting it. The £20-a-month subscription and the £2,000 workflow solve different problems.
How long until an automation pays for itself?
Count the hours the process eats now. A workflow that saves five hours a week at £25 an hour returns £6,500 a year against a £1,500 build. Payback inside three months is common; if the maths doesn’t work on paper, don’t build it.
Do I need to worry about GDPR?
Yes. Automated processing of personal data falls under UK GDPR, and you remain the data controller. Check your supplier’s ICO registration and ask where the data goes: which models see it, whether it trains anything, and how long it is kept.
What happens when the AI makes a mistake?
Well-built automations expect this: a human review step where a mistake would be expensive, logs so you can see what happened, and a fallback to the manual process. Ask to see all three in the demo.
