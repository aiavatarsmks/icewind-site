Essential technical processing

The site is served by Railway and delivered through Cloudflare, which also protects it from abuse. Fonts come from Google Fonts. These providers receive the technical information any web request carries, such as your IP address, browser type and the page requested, and keep short-lived server logs to run and secure the service. None of this requires consent, and none of it is used to profile you.

Cookieless visit statistics: Plausible

We use Plausible Analytics to see how the website as a whole is used: how many people visit, which pages they read, where they arrive from and whether the pages do their job. This is aggregate statistics about the site, not a record of you.

What Plausible receives. The page address without its query string, the referring website, campaign labels if the link carried them (for example utm_source), whether you arrived from a Google advert (the gclid parameter is recognised, used to label the visit as paid search, and then discarded), your browser and operating system, device type, approximate location derived from your IP address (country, region, city), how far you scrolled and how long the page stayed open. On the Business website for £499 page it also counts these moments: the offer details, case studies or form coming into view; clicks on the Get my website buttons by position; a click on the phone number; the first time a form field is used; and a successfully sent enquiry. Each of these is a named count only.

What Plausible never receives. Your name, email, phone number, message text or anything else you type; your full IP address; advertising identifiers. Form contents are not part of any event.

How it works without cookies. Plausible stores nothing on your device. To tell one visit from another within a day it hashes your IP address and browser signature with a secret that changes every 24 hours, and discards the raw values; the hash cannot be reversed or linked across days. Plausible Insights OÜ (Estonia) processes this on our behalf under a data processing agreement, on servers in the EU, and does not use the data for its own purposes.

Legal basis. PECR allows cookieless or cookie-based measurement without consent when its sole purpose is statistics about how a website is used in order to improve it, the data is not shared for other purposes, you are told clearly, and you can object simply and free of charge. That is the basis we rely on, and this section plus the control below are that notice and objection. To the extent any of this is personal data under UK GDPR, our lawful basis is our legitimate interest in understanding how the website performs.

Current state: counted (no cookies, nothing stored on your device).

Exclude this browser from our visit statistics

Excluding yourself saves one setting in this browser (plausible_ignore) so the statistics script is not loaded. When you send an enquiry form on the £499 page, a short-lived marker (iw-lead-pending, a timestamp only) is kept in this tab for a few minutes so that one enquiry is counted once, together with a note of which of the two forms was used (iw-lead-form); neither holds anything about you.

Cookieless page-speed beacon: Cloudflare Web Analytics

Cloudflare, which delivers the site, adds a small script that reports page load timings. It sends the page address without parameters, your browser family and version, and timing figures. It sets no cookies and keeps no identifier. Cloudflare processes this as our provider. You can block it with a content blocker; we are also able to switch it off at the network level if it adds nothing beyond Plausible.

Optional analytics that need your consent: Google Analytics 4, Microsoft Clarity and the OpenAI Ads pixel

These three tools use cookies and build a picture of individual visits, so they load only after you press Accept analytics. All three are loaded from the provider's own servers.

  • Google Analytics 4 (Google) measures visits, traffic sources and key actions such as sending an enquiry. It sets the cookies _ga and _ga_W2PWZGRFQH for up to two years. Google acts as our processor for the analytics data; aggregated reports do not expire, while event-level data is kept for the retention period set in our Google Analytics account.
  • Microsoft Clarity (Microsoft) records how a page is used, such as clicks, scrolling and mouse movement, and produces heatmaps and session replays. Every form field is masked, so nothing you type is recorded. Clarity sets the first-party cookies _clck (one year) and _clsk (one day) and, through its own domains, Microsoft cookies such as MUID. Microsoft keeps recordings for up to 30 days and may use the collected data under its own privacy statement, including to improve its products; this is one reason we ask before loading it. We only ever grant Clarity analytics consent, never advertising consent.
  • OpenAI Ads Measurement Pixel (OpenAI) measures whether an advert shown in ChatGPT led to a successfully sent enquiry on our £499 offer page. When you send that form and it is accepted by our server, the pixel reports one event, lead_created, together with a click reference from the advert if you arrived through one. It does not send OpenAI the text of your enquiry, your name, email address or phone number. The pixel sets first-party cookies (such as oppref and __obref) for up to one year and sends its events to OpenAI's servers. If you use ChatGPT, OpenAI may match the event to the advert you saw under its own privacy policy.

The OpenAI Ads pixel is the only advertising measurement tag on the site, and it loads only after Accept analytics. Sent enquiries are also counted as conversions in our Google Ads account through the Google Analytics 4 event, again only with your consent. We do not use remarketing tags or behavioural advertising profiling.

Changing or withdrawing consent

Use Change your cookie choice at any time. Choosing Reject after an earlier Accept stops Google Analytics on the spot, tells Clarity to delete its cookies and stop recording, and prevents all three consent-based tools, including the OpenAI Ads pixel, from loading on later pages. Google Analytics and OpenAI cookies already set are left for you to remove through your browser, or they expire on their own. You can also delete or block cookies in your browser settings; doing so may affect third-party widgets.

Third-party content

Some pages (the homepage, Start a project, Request an audit and Trust) embed a review widget from Clutch. It loads from clutch.co when the page opens and Clutch may set its own technical cookies on its domain, for example its anti-bot cookies. The £499 offer page links to Clutch but does not embed the widget. No other third-party content loads before you choose.
